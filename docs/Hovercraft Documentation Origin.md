\-The summer after my freshman year of high school, I attended an engineering summer camp, where I was instructed to build a motorized vehicle. While originally wanting to build a small hovercraft, my materials were constrained, and there did not exist a proper or motor powerful enough to inflate a miniature hovercraft skirt at the time. Instead, I had settled for an airboat with 4 motors hooked in series to each other and parallel to the two battery packs to power the craft. I handmade propellers and used foam board and duct tape to waterproof the structure. All in all, the project took a few hours and produced noticeable results. The craft was tested for seaworthiness with up to 2kg loaded on it before it sank, and the propellers produced noticeable airflow. Unfortunately, a true water run test was never conducted, as at the time, I lacked a large enough body of water.

\-Around December of 2025, I had the idea to continue the idea for this project and make an RC hovercraft. I looked up designs and began learning from articles online and AI what a hovercraft really is and how it worked. I learned about the skirt (bag skirts and finger skirts), different motor orientations/placements, and how you steered a hovercraft (rudder fins).

\-Around January of 2026, I asked myself, what if I made this hovercraft ridable for one person? I made the mistake of thinking that the overall design was relatively simple.

\- Over the next few months, I thought of multiple iterations and confirmed their practicality with AI. The first iteration was to use EDFs (Electric Ducted Fans) like ProjectAir to lift myself. This was when I first started to hear about terms like cubic flow per minute (CFM) and static pressure (SP). When discussing with AI, I was told electric ducted fans would not provide true lift and sufficient thrust for a lightweight craft and passenger. When asking why, I was told the CFM and static preassure generated from even several EDFs would be too low to lift a 200 pound craft. So progressed the several month journey to find a suitable airflow supplier for my hovercraft. After EDFs, I turned to electric leaf blowers. They produced enough CFM and static preassure for a lighweight craft, but gas-powered blowers were too loud, and quieter electric blowers had a very short maximum usage span with a larger increase in weight.
